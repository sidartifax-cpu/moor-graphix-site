Per Text. Per Violation. No cap on the number of violations per suit.

A TCPA violation doesn't require intent. It requires a text — sent to someone whose written consent you can't document, at a number they didn't opt into on a compliant form. The FCC knows what compliant opt-in looks like. Plaintiff attorneys know what it looks like.

No Documented Consent

Verbal agreement means nothing under TCPA. If the opt-in language wasn't on a compliant form at the time they submitted — you don't have consent you can prove.

10DLC Registration Denied

Carriers check for SMS Terms pages and Privacy Policy coverage during number registration. Missing either one and your application is rejected before you send a single message.

Class Action Exposure

One violation is rare. But a list of 500 customers texted without proper opt-in is 500 violations in a single send. That math compounds fast — and plaintiff attorneys know it.

What's Required by Law

Before you can legally send
a single business text, your
website needs 5 things.

Miss any one of these and your 10DLC number registration can be denied. Miss them after you've already started texting, and you're exposed.

01
A Privacy Policy that mentions SMS

Your existing Privacy Policy probably doesn't cover text messaging data. It needs to — specifically. Carriers and regulators look for explicit coverage of how SMS data is collected, stored, and used. A generic privacy policy doesn't pass the check.

Privacy Policy Update
02
A dedicated SMS Terms & Conditions page

This spells out what you'll text, how often, and what it costs recipients. Carriers check for this page during 10DLC number registration. Without it, your registration is denied. This is not a section on another page — it requires its own URL.

New Dedicated Page
03
Opt-in language on every form

Every contact form, booking form, or lead capture on your site needs compliant opt-in copy before the submit button. "By submitting, you agree to receive texts from [Business] at the number provided…" — that language has to be there. Exactly. On every form that captures phone numbers.

Form Copy — All Locations
04
Opt-out instructions visible to subscribers

Customers must know how to stop receiving messages. "Reply STOP to unsubscribe" isn't just courtesy — it's a federal requirement under TCPA. This must appear where customers opt in and be included in initial messages sent after signup.

Opt-Out Language
05
Message frequency disclosure

"Message frequency may vary" or the actual cadence you plan to text — this disclosure has to appear where customers opt in. Vague is okay, but absent is not. Carriers and compliance reviewers check for this during the registration process.

Frequency Disclosure
