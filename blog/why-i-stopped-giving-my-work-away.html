Why I Stopped Giving My Work Away

Sid Washington  ·  July 22, 2026

For years I gave my time away for free — logos, discounted packages, strategy sessions that ran hours over. Here's the lesson every creative eventually has to face: generosity without structure isn't generosity. It's slow erosion.

For years I gave my time away. Free logos. Discounted brand packages. Strategy sessions that ran two hours over because I cared more about your vision than my invoice. If you had a dream and I believed in it, I showed up. No contract necessary.

That generosity came from my heart. I don't regret a single hour of it.

But here's a truth every creative eventually has to face. Generosity without structure isn't generosity. It's slow erosion.

The lesson nobody warns you about

You start out wanting to help everybody. A nonprofit needs a logo, you do it for cost. An artist needs a website, you do it for exposure. A friend of a friend needs a brand strategy, you do it for a favor you'll never collect on.

Then one day you look up and you've built everyone else's dream on the back of your own unpaid labor.

I've been designing and building brands for over 20 years. I've sat in the dean's chair at a design college. I've taught kids how to think like builders. I've run a multi-brand company from the ground up. And still, the hardest lesson wasn't strategy or craft. It was this:

"You cannot pour from a cup you never let fill."

The math creatives don't want to do

Every hour you give away is an hour you didn't spend on the business that keeps your lights on. Every discount you extend to "help someone out" is a discount you're quietly taking from your own family, your own future, your own legacy.

This isn't about greed. It's about sustainability. A creative who burns out broke helps nobody. A creative who builds a stable, thriving practice can help for decades.

Ask yourself. Who does it serve if you disappear in five years because you gave it all away for free?

What changes now

Going forward, every project, every consultation, every design, every strategy session, every creative service runs through paid engagements. No exceptions made out of guilt. No discounts extended out of obligation.

This isn't a wall between me and the people I serve. It's a foundation strong enough to keep serving them.

When you invest in a creative, you're not buying a file or a deliverable. You're investing in years of experience, pattern recognition, strategic thinking, and a partner who's staked their own name on your success. That has a price, and it should.

To every creative reading this

If you're still giving your gift away for free, hoping it turns into something later, hear me. Your talent is not a favor. It is not a hobby. It is a business, and businesses that don't charge don't survive to build the legacy they set out to build.

Set your price. Hold your price. Let the work speak, and let the invoice follow.

Grind now so you can shine later.

— Sid Washington, Moor Graphix
